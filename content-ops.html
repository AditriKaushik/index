1. Vacancy radar WHAT WE ARE WRITING FOR

Every content decision starts here. A recruitment cycle enters the radar the day a calendar or notification is published, and leaves it the day the final result is out. Nothing is commissioned for an exam that is not on this board.

Source-of-truth rule. No row below has been checked against the issuing authority. Each was reconciled on 15 Sep 2026 across independent secondary reports, and carries the confidence that pass produced:
  • Reconciled two or more independent sources agree on the figure or date. Safe to plan against; still not safe to print.
  • Disputed sources contradict each other. Blocked for any date-bearing asset until the official notice settles it.
  • Calendar-only a tentative window from a published calendar, with no confirmation. Treat as a planning placeholder — the SSC CGL 2026 row is the cautionary case: the calendar said May–June, the exam moved to 30 Sep–30 Oct.
Each exam name links to the portal that owns the answer. Before a cycle is commissioned or any date reaches a promotional asset, the owner opens that link, confirms the row and re-stamps it. A row not re-checked within 14 days goes amber on the dashboard and blocks publication of date-bearing assets.

2.4 Schedule — when it ships

Every item is scheduled backwards from the exam date, never forwards from the day work starts. The T− ladder in section 6 is the default; the planner converts it into real dates. Two scheduling constraints override everything else:

  • Freeze at T−2. No new content of any type is published in the final 48 hours except the exam-day instruction asset. Late material creates anxiety and generates support load without changing outcomes.
  • Print lock at T−30. Anything going to press — kits, OMR papers — is locked and approved 30 days out, because print plus dispatch to centres is a 14–21 day path that cannot be compressed.

Cross-cutting requirements for every type

  • Metadata before content. No item is created without exam, cycle, tier, stage, subject, topic, difficulty, language, author, version and intended publish slot. Untagged content cannot be found, reused, or measured, and it is what makes a library expensive instead of an asset.
  • Reuse before authoring. The brief must record what was searched in the existing bank and why it was insufficient. Target: at least 40% of Tier C volume served from reuse.
  • Single source of numbers. Vacancy counts, dates, fees and cut-offs come from the radar record, not from the author's memory or a competitor's page.
  • Versioning. Published items are never edited in place; a correction creates v2 with a changelog line and, where the item is date-bearing, a visible "updated on" stamp.

Loop control

  • Two-loop limit. An item may go around create → audit → correct twice. A third failed audit escalates to the Head of Content, who either reassigns the item or kills it. Endless polishing is the commonest way a content calendar quietly slips.
  • Auditor independence. The auditor is never the author and never reports to the author. On a small desk, cross-audit between subject leads; the rule that matters is that no one signs off their own work.
  • Emergency lane. Event-driven items (shift analysis, answer key, notification explainer) run a compressed pipeline: create → audit → approve, with the editor review folded into the audit and a 3-hour total SLA. They are marked provisional on publication and re-reviewed within 24 hours.
  • Stop-the-line. A factual error found in live content outranks all scheduled work. Correction is published within 6 hours, the item is versioned, and the defect is entered in the trend review.

8. Information dashboard — blueprint THE SPECIFICATION

One dashboard, seven screens, four layers. It answers four questions and is designed to answer nothing else: what is coming, are we covered, is it moving, and is it any good. Every panel below names its source, its refresh and the decision it drives — a panel that drives no decision does not get built.

The two failure modes to guard against. First, building screens before the metric definitions are written down — you get a dashboard nobody trusts because every panel can be argued with. Second, tracking production but never joining it to consumption, so the team optimises for items shipped rather than items attempted. Both are cheap to prevent at phase 1 and expensive to retrofit at phase 5.
